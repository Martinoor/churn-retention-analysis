Cancellation is a decision,
not a decline.

What happens before a subscriber cancels on a music-streaming service, and where a retention team should intervene.

Martino Orioli 17,499,636 events 19,140 users 51 days

Modelling developed with a teammate for the 2025/26 Kaggle churn competition, which our team won. The behavioural analysis, findings and recommendations presented here are my own.

01 The question

Retention pays only if we know whom to reach, when, and how.

The competition scored models on balanced accuracy over a held-out set. That ranks models; it does not tell a retention team what to do on Monday.

  1. Q1

    Is churn predictable?

    Or does it look random until the moment someone cancels?

  2. Q2

    From which signals, and how early?

    A signal that appears the day before cancellation leaves no time to act.

  3. Q3

    Where does retention effort pay back?

    Contacting every user costs more than it recovers.

Two constraints shaped the method

The prediction window is not in the data

The competition asked for churn in the 10 days after 20 November, but the logs end on that date. Features are therefore built up to an anchor date, and the label is cancellation after it.

Nothing before 1 October is observed

A user's first logged event is not their sign-up date. Tenure is measured from registration, and the run-up analysis uses only users whose full history is visible: 1,108 of 4,271 cancellers.

03 The first look

The expected fade was not there. Cancellers looked busier.

Aligned on the day they cancelled, and set against retained users on random dates, cancellers ran at 18.7 events a day four weeks out, against 6.9: nearly three times as active, and climbing into a spike.

Before acting on this, one question: is the comparison fair?

04 A fair comparison

Matched on day and plan, the gap disappears.

  • Seasonality

    Everyone listens more as these weeks go on (+9% for retained users over the same dates).

  • Plan

    Cancellers are more often paid subscribers, and paid subscribers listen more.

  • Presence

    A cancellation requires a visit. A random day for a retained user is often a day with no visit.

05 What they do

Nothing in the experience gets worse before a cancellation.

Cancellers and matched users shift their activity in the same way. Skips move from 12.5 to 12.2 per 100 events (matched: 12.6 to 12.3), errors stay at 0.10, and downgrade-page visits move from 0.83 to 0.89 (matched: 0.74 to 0.79).

The day of cancellation is excluded: it contains the cancellation flow itself, which would otherwise appear as behaviour.

06 Who cancels more

Heavy users cancel more because they visit more, not because each visit is riskier.

Among paid subscribers, the most frequent visitors come 11.9× as often as the least frequent and cancel 5.5× as often. Each visit carries some chance of cancellation; engaged users simply have more visits.

08 Who to contact

A one-line rule gets most of the way. The model adds 11 points.

The rule “paid, and seen in the last 3 days” flags 33% of active users and reaches 55% of those who cancel within 10 days. Ranking the same number of users by model score reaches 66%.

09 What it is worth

Whether a campaign pays depends on a number we do not have yet.

The value of contacting users turns on the save rate: the share of contacted cancellers who stay. No amount of historical data can supply it; only an experiment can.

10 What it means

Act at the moment of decision, not on a predicted decline.

  • An engagement-decline alert would never fire

    There is no decline to detect.

  • Activity-based campaigns reach the safest users

    They mostly find frequent visitors, who are the least likely to cancel on any given visit.

  • The cancellation path is where intent is visible

    Most cancellations are reached from the downgrade page.

PROPOSED TEST

A save offer on the downgrade and cancellation path

Offer
Pause the subscription, a lower-priced plan, or a time-limited discount.
Design
Randomise users entering the flow into offer and holdout groups.
Primary metric
Subscribers still active 60 days later, not only “did not cancel today”.
Guardrails
Revenue per user, and cancellations that are delayed rather than prevented.
What it yields
The measured save rate that every budget calculation here depends on.

11 Limits

What this analysis can and cannot claim.

  • The data is very likely simulated

    The schema matches Udacity's public Sparkify dataset, and 30% of cancellations are reached directly from an advert page, which a real app would not allow. Some results may be built into the data; the method is what transfers.

  • Only explicit cancellations are visible

    Users who stop listening without cancelling count as retained.

  • Observational, one season, one anchor date

    Every recommendation is a hypothesis to test, and the save rate is assumed.

Next steps, in order

  1. Run the save-offer test on the cancellation path.
  2. Hold any targeting model to the one-line rule and fund it for the 11 points it adds.
  3. Test advert frequency for free users, the one experience lever with signal within plan.
  4. Collect what the logs lack: billing dates, plan price, cancellation reasons.
  5. Add a behavioural churn label (no activity for N days) and repeat the analysis.
  6. Track cancellations per 1,000 sessions, so more usage is not mistaken for more risk.
